King in Yellow

From Warhammer 40k - Lexicanum
(Redirected from Yellow King)
Jump to: navigation, search

The King in Yellow is a mysterious figure connected to the heretical Chaos-dabbling sect known as the Cognitae.[1]

History

Believed by some to be a figure of mere folk legend, the legend of the King and his inner circle known as The Eight have existed for many centuries. He is an enemy of both Gregor Eisenhorn and Gideon Ravenor, who have battled him for many years. The King's identity is unknown. His purpose is also unknown, except that it involves the Immaterium. Apparently, one of his goals is to find out the true name of the Emperor in order to obtain power over Him, and this has led him on searches from the Planet of the Sorcerers to Sicarus to the Black Library. Eisenhorn has pledged to hunt down and destroy the King before he completes his ultimate objective.[1] Besides the Cognitae, the King in Yellow has amassed an army of "angels"[Note 1] and "custodians"[Note 2]. The King also has a number of cloned Blanks in his service.[2c]

Besides those loyal to the Imperium, the King in Yellow has also amassed a great many other enemies who either seek to stop him from completing his objectives or steal his power for their own ends. These include the Alpha Legion[2a], Word Bearers[2a], Emperor's Children[2a], Night Lords[2b], Thousand Sons[2d], Iron Warriors[2b], rogue Cognitae agent Mam Mordaunt[2b], and the Eldar, who have mobilized Craftworlds Ulthwe, Alaitoc, Elathec, Nyatho, and Olhn-Tann against him[2f].

After entering the pocket dimension known as the City of Dust, Alizebeth Bequin was nearly taken before the King in Yellow, but she used Enuncia to escape. Bequin and her own entourage recovered a book whose entire contents were a single name which was apparently the identity of the King in Yellow. The first words in the book were "Constantin Valdor".[2c]

Trivia

The following information is not to be regarded as officially sanctioned and/ or is not written from an in-universe point of view. A detailed explanation of the term "Trivia" as used in the Lexicanum can be found here.

The King in Yellow is a collection of short stories written by Robert W. Chambers in 1895. The stories involve a fictional play of the same name and speak of a mysterious entity which brings madness and ruin.

Notes

  • Note 1: When inside the City of Dust, Alizebeth Bequin sees "flights of angels", as well as "huge statues, each one a giant winged man of noble aspect hewn from alabaster", all of which bore the likeness of Comus Nocturnus, a winged Blood Angels Space Marine that Bequin met in the Below of Queen Mab. Shortly before meeting Nocturnus, Bequin saw the skeletal remains of what she assumed to be a Space Marine that also seemed to have had biological wings in life.[2e] Whether or not the "flights of angels", Nocturnus, and the dead winged being are connected is unknown.
  • Note 2: The King in Yellow is also shown to have "custodians" in his service, spelled and capitalized thusly.[2c] It is unknown whether or not these "custodians" are warriors of the Adeptus Custodes, as the title has a number of other meanings, and Dan Abnett has used the title before to refer to characters who are not Custodes.
