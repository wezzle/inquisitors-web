Characters who debuted in the Bequin novel series. See also the character sheets for Eisenhorn and Ravenor, which overlap this series.    open/close all folders  
Beta Bequin 
Alizebeth "Beta" Bequin
"This, I think, will be my life story, and it will start here. You will not learn much from me, or you will learn everything. I have not yet decided which."
A orphan with pariah abilities born in the city Queen Mab on the planet Sancour. She was trained by the Maze Undue to be a servant of the Inquisition. When the facility is attacked and destroyed, she finds herself embroiled in a galaxy-spanning conflict, with multiple powerful factions seeking her out for their own ends. Action Survivor: Though trained to serve the Inquisition as something akin to a Sister of Silence, she's still quite a sheltered young woman when the series begins, and survives the attack on the Maze Undue and subsequent events as much by luck as by skill. She gradually grows more skillful and discerning over the course of the trilogy, and somewhat ruefully remarks in Penitent that she might have the makings of an Inquisitor in her own right.
Anti-Magic: As a Pariah, psychic powers and sorcery don't work in her presence, and she creates a blank spot where even the strongest psykers can't act or see into simply by being there.
Doppelgänger Gets Same Sentiment: Downplayed. It's revealed at the end of Pariah that she's a clone of Eisenhorn's longtime retinue member Alizebeth Bequin, who had suffered Death of Personality in Hereticus and then her body went missing. Eisenhorn was quietly and hopelessly in love with the original, and has a similar instinctive reaction to the sight of Beta (since she looks almost exactly like her "mother" did when he first knew her), but seems to view her as more of a surrogate daughter.
Living MacGuffin: There are at least seven different factions that want to get their hands on her for various reasons. Some of them want her because she’s a pariah who can use Enuncia, some want her because of her connections to the Maze Undue and by extension, the Cognitae, and several want to use her as a weapon against an eighth faction. Toward the end of the book, she privately laments that she’s become the grail which all these people are questing for.
Master of Disguise: Everyone raised in the Maze Undue is this as part of their training to be Inquisition agents.
Meaningful Name: Her nickname "Beta", because she's a clone of Alizebeth Bequin, the "alpha" version.
Modified Clone: Beta is a clone of Alizebeth Bequin but not an exact copy: additional genetic material was used in her creation. Eisenhorn describes Beta as "her daughter, technically".
Parental Abandonment: She explains in the first chapter of Pariah that, so she was told by people she herself considers Unreliable Expositors, she was found wandering in the marshes after her mother died of disease, and brought to the Scholam Orbus to be raised. She is in fact a clone of the deceased Inquisitorial acolyte Alizebeth Bequin, and therefore never had a birth family at all.
Power Limiter: She wears a bracelet supplied by the Maze Undue that allows her to dial up or dial down her blankness with the twist of a knob, and she doesn't hesitate to do so whichever direction it benefits her: she often switches it on and off multiple times in the same encounter to surprise hostile psykers.
Renner Lightburn 
Renner Lightburn
A man who lives on the holloways of Queen Mab, performing any tasks asked of him as atonement for his sins. He was hired by an unknown party to escort Bequin to safety. He forms a friendship with her that leads him to joining her cause.
Friendless Background: Owing to his status, he doesn't have any friends. When Bequin seeks him out to thank him for his help and describes him as a friend, that's enough to convince him to join her.
Laser-Guided Amnesia:  Ravenor erases his memory of his time with Bequin during the events of the first book. Beta reacquaints herself with him in the second book.
The Penance: Renner is a Curst, a type of penitent which lives in poverty and carries out any task asked of them, symbolically taking on another’s burden to atone for their own sins. They write their crimes on their skin in ink as a visual representation of the burdens they carry: Lightburn’s crimes cover most of his body. His current burden is taking Beta to Mam Mordaunt. Near the end of Pariah, he reveals his original crime: as a temple guard, he gave shelter to a scared teenage girl being chased by a mob, unaware that she was an unsanctioned psyker.
Ship Tease: Renner assumes he and Beta were lovers due to the lengths she went to re-establish contact with him.
